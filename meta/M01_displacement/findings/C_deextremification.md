# Findings C: alignment flattens emotional extremity, corpus-wide

Split out of `C_to_O_registered_letters.md` on 2026-08-12 (RH's commission), rewritten the same day to be readable on its own: the hypotheses are stated in plain terms rather than by registration number. `REGISTRATIONS.md` remains authoritative for every number; the registration files hold the frozen statistical detail.

## What was asked

Registration C put three rival pictures of alignment's effect on emotional vocabulary to the same corpus-wide test. **Sweetening** (the registration's H1): alignment removes negatively charged words and promotes positive ones — the model becomes *nicer*. **De-extremification** (H2): alignment removes emotionally EXTREME words regardless of whether they are positive or negative, and promotes neutral ones — the model becomes *flatter*. **Dominance flattening** (H3): the same flattening, but on the dominance scale (how much power or control a word connotes) rather than emotional charge. The three make different predictions on the same words: under sweetening, "wonderful" should rise; under de-extremification, "wonderful" is as extreme as "horrible" and both should fall.

## What was found

**De-extremification is confirmed; the model gets flatter, not nicer.** The contrast on absolute valence — do emotionally extreme words lose probability to neutral ones — came out positive at +0.025 after residualising (that is, after removing the part of the movement already explained by how probable each word was to begin with), p 0.0012. **Dominance flattening is dead**: the same contrast on the dominance scale shows nothing. **Sweetening was never adjudicated**: its verdict arm stayed blind and was never emitted, so C says nothing for or against it.

## The limit that travels

C's artifact record is two `.txt` transcripts of the session, nothing machine-readable — a producer-debt case (Class 1). **Quote C only alongside E**, which replicated the de-extremification result on a blind stratum with a real producer writing real artifacts. C's dead dominance result was later reproduced, unplanned, by the K word-rating campaign — a negative control nobody designed.
