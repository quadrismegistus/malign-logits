# Registration R (working title): Is the swap meaningful?

**STATUS: PLAIN-ENGLISH DRAFT FOR RH's SIGN-OFF. Nothing frozen. No test has been run.**

This is the short version, written to be refused or amended before anyone writes the formal one. Per the standing rule: you get the hypotheses in English, what would confirm each, the n, and what is being left out, before it hardens.

## The setup, in one paragraph

At a transgressive site, alignment pushes one word down (the **faller**) and pulls another up (the **riser**). Arithmetically the riser has substituted for the faller. That is a fact about the distribution, not a discovery. What we do not know is whether the substitution means anything: whether the riser stands in some interpretable relation to the faller, or whether the mass simply landed somewhere arbitrary.

To find out, three AI coders were shown pairs of words in their prompt context and asked what relation, if any, holds between them. Each real pair was matched to a **control**: the same faller, paired with a word that did *not* rise. The coders were never told which was which, and could not have known.

## The one filter, applied before anything else

The coding instrument has a mechanical rule: if either word is not a content word in that slot, the answer is forced to "no relation." Risers and controls may differ in how often they are content words, and if they do, every relation would appear to rise at real pairs for a reason that has nothing to do with meaning.

**So: only pairs where all three coders agree both words are content words enter any test.** The number excluded is reported.

## The primary question

**Do real pairs get "no relation" less often than their controls?**

- **Yes** means the substitution is semantically motivated. Something connects the word that fell to the word that rose.
- **No** means the swap is arithmetic only, and there is nothing to interpret. This is the single outcome that would refute the psychoanalytic reading of alignment.

## The secondary question: what kind of relation?

If the swap is motivated, the coders' labels sort into two families.

- **REPLACEMENT relations**, where the riser stands in for the faller: same act at another magnitude, a more or less specific term, a euphemism, a shifted target, an act becoming a voicing of feeling, an opposite.
- **COMPANY relations**, where the riser sits alongside the faller: a later or earlier moment of the same event, or a different act by the same person in the same scene.

This gives four outcomes, and each means something different:

| | REPLACEMENT rises | it does not |
|---|---|---|
| **COMPANY rises** | **H3. Dreamwork in general.** The model relates the two words by whatever means are available. Both of Freud's mechanisms are in play and neither dominates. | **H2. Contexture.** The model does not replace the forbidden word, it goes on to something else in the scene. Genre change, already documented in this campaign. |
| **it does not** | **H1. Substitution.** The model reaches for a stand-in. This is displacement in the narrow sense the project first described. | **H4. Nothing.** The swap has no interpretable relation. The refutation. |

## Operationalizing the table: exactly which labels count where

The coders tick all relations that apply, most important first, from ten labels. Those ten map onto the two families as follows, with nothing left over and nothing counted twice.

**REPLACEMENT family** (the riser stands in for the faller):

    SAME_ACT       the same act at another magnitude
    SPECIFICITY    one term a kind or part of the other
    EUPHEMISM      the same referent in another register
    METONYMY       the act holds, the target shifts to an adjacent one
    AFFECT         an act becomes an emotion or a voicing of one
    OPPOSITION     opposite directions along one shared axis

**COMPANY family** (the riser sits alongside the faller):

    SEQUENCE       an earlier or later moment of one unfolding event
    CO_ACT         a different act by the same agent in the same scene

**Neither family, and both are reported separately:**

    OTHER          a real connection the list does not name. Counts as
                   MOTIVATED for the primary question, and toward neither
                   family for the secondary one.
    NONE           no relation. The primary question is entirely about this
                   label's rate.

### How an item is counted

**An item counts toward REPLACEMENT if it carries at least one label from that family, and toward COMPANY if it carries at least one from that one. An item carrying both counts toward both.**

This is deliberate and it is the thing that makes the table work. The two rows of the table are **two separate yes/no contrasts**, not a division of items into groups. So no item ever has to be assigned to one family, and the instrument's own instruction, "do not force a single choice," is respected rather than overridden. Earlier drafts of this design tried to take shares of the two families and broke, because a pair can be both and the shares then do not add up.

Each contrast is run the same way: for each matched pair of items, does the real one carry the family and the control not, or the reverse. The test is over those disagreements.

## The theoretical echo, which is exact and worth stating

The ten labels sort on two dimensions at once, and the second one is Jakobson's.

The first dimension is what the model **did**: replace a word (selection) or proceed past it (combination). That is the table's two rows. The second is the **principle** by which the two words are associated: similarity or contiguity. That is Jakobson's metaphoric and metonymic poles.

|  | **similarity** | **contiguity** |
|---|---|---|
| **selection** (replacement) | SAME_ACT, SPECIFICITY, EUPHEMISM, OPPOSITION | **METONYMY, AFFECT** |
| **combination** (company) | — | SEQUENCE, CO_ACT |

Three things follow, and the third is the interesting one.

1. The upper-left cell is **metaphor**: a substitution licensed by resemblance. Condensation.
2. The lower-right cell is pure **contexture**: no substitution, association by adjacency alone. Genre change.
3. The upper-right cell is **a substitution licensed by contiguity**, which is displacement in Lacan's precise sense, and the empty lower-left confirms it is not an accident of our sorting. **This is where kill to scream lives**, coded AFFECT and CO_ACT together, and it is the only cell that requires both of Jakobson's axes to describe.

The instrument was not built to produce this square. It groups its labels by replacement versus accompaniment and says nothing about similarity or contiguity anywhere in its text. The square falls out of the definitions when they are read against Jakobson, which is a modest kind of evidence that the coding scheme is tracking something real rather than a vocabulary we invented.

**A caution that belongs beside it.** H1 and H2 test the rows, not the cells. Distinguishing metaphor from displacement inside the REPLACEMENT row would need the four-versus-two split above, and those two labels are the rarest in the set. That is a further question, not this registration's, and it should not be smuggled into the reading of H1.

## The separate question: is it softening?

The relation labels say what kind of substitution. They say nothing about direction. A second field records whether the risen word is **milder** than the fallen one, equal, stronger, or not comparable at all.

This is not a fifth hypothesis. It is the difference between a finding and a psychoanalytic finding:

- motivated substitution **and milder** = censorship. Dreamwork proper.
- motivated substitution **and stronger** = escalation, which is not repression.
- motivated substitution **and flat** = the model finds related words without softening. Real, and not a symptom.

So whichever of H1 to H4 comes out, the intensity result is reported beside it and governs what may be claimed.

## What we predict, and who predicted it

Stated in advance so it cannot be fitted afterward.

- **H3 most likely.** The flagship pair, kill to scream, carries both families at once. Alignment has never shown one mechanism anywhere else in this campaign.
- **H1 second.** If softening is real, the model reaches for a stand-in, and pure narrative continuation should not rise with it.
- **H4 third, and held open seriously.** Once the content-word filter removes the mechanical share, what remains may be small.
- **H2 alone least likely.** It would mean the model never replaces, only continues, which contradicts the finding that mass lands on specific nameable words.

## What this does NOT test, and why

This is the section the last registration did not have.

- **Direction of specificity.** The instrument records that two words differ in generality but not which way. "The riser is more general" cannot be tested with what was collected.
- **OPPOSITION.** No theory on the table predicts the model reaches for an opposite. It is reported with no prediction attached.
- **Speech act beyond what has been done.** One value on one stratum has been tested. The rest of that field is untouched here.
- **Anything per slot or per domain.** One test over the whole population. Slicing comes later or not at all.
- **Human validation.** The coders are three model families agreeing with each other at moderate rates. Agreement among models is not correctness.

## What we have already seen, stated so the credential is honest

We have looked at the pooled distribution of the labels, mixing real pairs and controls together, and at how often the three coders tie. We have **not** computed any comparison between real pairs and controls, for any field, at any seat. So the direction of every hypothesis above is genuinely unknown.

But the labels, the two families, and the shape of this design were all chosen by people who had seen those pooled numbers. **The credential is: no contrast has been computed, so no direction is known. It is not: this design was specified blind.** Both sentences, or the first one claims more than the facts support.
