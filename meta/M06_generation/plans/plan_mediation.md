# Plan: the M01-M06 mediation test

## Why this exists

The two campaigns' metric families do not covary. At pair grain (n=38), the
alignment delta in M01's interiority axis predicts none of M06's deltas:
rho +0.093 for passage surprisal (p .58), +0.078 total_drift (p .64), +0.194
mean_drift (p .24). Meanwhile M06's own metrics predict each other at
rho +0.787 (p 5e-09). Within-family coherence is detectable at n=38; the
cross-family link is not.

That is a correlation between two SUMMARY statistics, which is the weakest
possible bridge and the one most easily killed by noise in either. It does not
answer the question underneath it, which RH put as the question a reviewer asks
first: **alignment lowers passage surprisal by 0.559 nats -- which words carry
that drop, and are they the words M01 identifies as movers?**

If they are, M01 and M06 are one operation observed at two scales. If they are
not, the campaign has two findings that happen to co-occur, and the paper must
say so rather than implying a mechanism it never tested.

## The instrument, and why it is better than the one first proposed

The first draft of this plan was going to re-score passages with gpt2 (the
reference model `m06_f15_forced.py` uses) and decompose its surprisal. RH
pointed out that self and cross surprisal scores already exist for these
passages. They do, and they are a strictly better instrument.

In the `passage` corpus, every undisturbed text is scored by BOTH members of
its pair: 1,142,944 self rows and 1,142,944 cross rows, and for each
(model, prompt, sample_idx) both scorers are present at equal count.

    text T generated by model M in pair (B, A)
      s_B(T)   base's per-token surprisal on T
      s_A(T)   aligned's per-token surprisal on T

**Same text, same tokens, two models.** The per-token difference is a LEVEL
measurement with composition held exactly fixed BY CONSTRUCTION -- not
controlled for, not matched, but identical. There is no frequency confound in
it, because every word is its own control: the same word, in the same context,
under two distributions.

That is the cleanest form this question can take, and it needs no new
generation and no new scoring.

## The decomposition

Passage surprisal per word in role r is `sum_w f_r(w) s_r(w)`, for f the share
of word tokens and s the mean surprisal of that word. The aligned-minus-base
difference splits symmetrically:

    Delta = sum_w (f_a - f_b) * sbar(w)        COMPOSITION: different words
          + sum_w fbar(w) * (s_a - s_b)        LEVEL: same word, cheaper

with `sbar = (s_a + s_b)/2` and `fbar = (f_a + f_b)/2`. In this Shapley-symmetric
form the two terms sum to Delta exactly, with no residual interaction.

**GATE R (reconciliation).** The producer must verify numerically that
composition + level equals the observed Delta to within 1e-9, and must print
both terms and the residual. A decomposition whose parts do not sum to the
whole is mis-specified, and the failure is silent otherwise.

**GATE U (the unit).** The artifact's `mean_surprisal` is per TOKEN; this
decomposition is per WORD. They differ by tokens-per-word, which alignment may
itself change. The producer must report the token-grain Delta, the word-grain
Delta, and mean tokens-per-word in each arm, and must state which grain each
claim is in. (A count is not a unit; four correct counts have been given four
wrong unit words in this campaign.)

## Declared readings

**M1 -- LEVEL, DIRECTIONAL. The core mediation claim.**
On identical text, M01 fallers are MORE surprising under aligned than under
base; M01 risers are LESS surprising. Both halves must hold. This is M01's
slot-level claim re-expressed as a claim about the aligned distribution in
general: if alignment demotes a word only in the transgressive slots M01
measured, it will not show here; if it demotes the word wherever it appears, it
must.
Refuted by: a null mover contrast, or either half reversed.

**M2 -- COMPOSITION, DIRECTIONAL.**
Aligned generations under-use M01 fallers and over-use M01 risers relative to
base generations, beyond a frequency-matched baseline.
Refuted by: null or reversed after frequency matching.

**M3 -- SHARE, DIRECTIONAL, DECLARED NULL.**
M01's mover set carries no more of the total surprisal drop than a
frequency-matched non-mover set of the same size. Enrichment ratio <= 1.
Refuted by: enrichment >= 1.5 with a bootstrap CI excluding 1.

M1/M2 and M3 are not in contradiction, and the distinction is the point. M1/M2
are about DIRECTION on the mover set; M3 is about the SHARE of the aggregate
drop those movers carry. Alignment can move exactly the words M01 says it moves
while those words are too rare to account for a 0.559 nat shift across a whole
passage.

**That combination is this seat's modal expectation, stated here so it can
fail**: the movers move as M01 says, and carry a small share of the passage
effect. If that is what comes back, the finding is that the two campaigns
observe one operation at two scales, with the passage effect mostly carried by
vocabulary M01 never measured -- which is a result, not a disappointment.

**Direction check on the declarer.** This seat's bias is inflationary and its
interest runs toward mediation being TRUE (one argument beats two). M3 is
therefore declared against interest. But the memory that applies here is
`nomination is not conviction`: this seat's convictions have survived and its
acquittals have not, so a declared null from this seat is the reading most
likely to be wrong, and M3 gets the bootstrap rather than an eyeball.

## Gates before any reading

**GATE T (tokenizer identity).** Equal token count is NECESSARY, NOT
SUFFICIENT; a count match is not evidence of correspondence. Measured:
237,804 of 238,400 texts (99.75%) have identical `n` under both scorers, and
596 do not. The mismatches are `BAAI/Aquila2-7B` (592) and
`croissantllm/CroissantLLMChat-v0.1` (4).
Required: for each retained pair, decode a sample of texts under both
tokenizers and assert token-string identity position by position. Exclude by
PAIR, not by row -- a pair whose tokenizers disagree on 1% of texts is not
trustworthy on the other 99%.

**GATE P (pair-level, never pooled).** `movement` is keyed by
(base, aligned, prompt, word), so a mover is a per-pair fact. The join is per
pair and every summary reports its members' spread. A pooled mover lexicon
would import the pooling defect that has already hidden one member six times in
this campaign.

**GATE V (movers exist in passages).** M01's lexicon comes from its own
transgressive sites. The producer must report how many mover types actually
occur in passage text, and their token share, BEFORE any share claim. If movers
are 0.2% of tokens, M3 is arithmetically foreordained and must be reported as
such rather than as a finding.

## Population and rule

- Undisturbed (`forced_word = ''`) texts of the `passage` corpus.
- 44 pairs appear in both `movement` and the passage corpus; minus Aquila, and
  minus croissantllm if GATE T fails it.
- Mover rule: `movement.py`'s CANONICAL rule (min_prob 0.003, fall_ratio 0.5,
  delta 0.003, null_test on), which is the only rule whose risers are tested
  against the renormalisation null. **Fallers are NOT null-tested under
  CANONICAL and nothing here may describe fallers as though they were.**
- Word key: `movement.word` is lowercase-dominant but not exclusively
  (14,739 of 161,784 types contain uppercase). The producer must MEASURE the
  join rate under its chosen normalisation and report it, not assume a
  convention.

## Fences

- The join is at word TYPE across different prompts, so this is a lexicon-level
  claim. A null in M1 would not show that alignment leaves the lexicon alone,
  only that M01's slot-derived mover list does not predict passage-level
  surprisal shifts.
- Lexicon provenance: M01's movers are derived from BASE-model probabilities at
  M01's sites. Mover status may correlate with base-corpus frequency in
  passages; measure that correlation before interpreting any enrichment.
  (A lexicon read off one arm measures that arm's vocabulary.)
- Cross-scoring means the aligned model scores text it did not generate, which
  is off-policy for it. That is the design, not a defect, but it means the LEVEL
  term is a property of the two DISTRIBUTIONS, not of either arm's behaviour.
- Single pass, one seat, no cross-seat reproduction. Ungraded until a second
  seat reproduces (per [5503]).
