# Findings O: the substitution travels across languages; the affect signature does not

Split out of `C_to_O_registered_letters.md` on 2026-08-12 (RH's commission), rewritten the same day to be readable on its own: the hypotheses are stated in plain terms rather than by registration number. `REGISTRATIONS.md` remains authoritative for every number; the registration files hold the frozen statistical detail.

## What was asked

The same content in two languages. Three questions, each required to hold in BOTH the English arm and the Chinese arm to count: does the substitution structure appear (freed mass re-landing on nameable words rather than dispersing — `tail_excess` negative in both languages); does emotional extremity fall in both (the de-extremification signature); does arousal fall in both?

## What was found

**The substitution travels.** Negative `tail_excess` in both arms — English 2,277 cells for versus 365 against, Chinese 2,463 versus 141 — under the registration's Chinese-origin bound (a declared control for translation artifacts). **The affect signature does not travel.** Both extremity and arousal come out English-confirming with the Chinese arms at clean coin-flip nulls (648/650 and 652/646). Not reversals: nulls.

The paper's sentence: **the substitution travels, the affect does not.** The structural operation — one word standing in for another — is language-general; the affective flattening documented in C, D2 and E is, on this evidence, a fact about English (or about what alignment training saw in English).

## The limit that travels

The Chinese-origin bound governs all quotation. The zh nulls are nulls, not reversals — no sentence may claim the affect signature inverts in Chinese. The combined statistics are floors, as in N.
