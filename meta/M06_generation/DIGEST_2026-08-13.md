## What stands

**F15 retried on the passage corpus** (`f15_on_passages.md`). All four of F15's claims replicate at the pair grain, n=38. Alignment smooths reference surprisal (−0.53 nats, 35/38 pairs), reduces drift (34/38), drains the breakdown quadrant into unmarked **and metonymic**, and compresses uniformly across site types. The upgrade: F15 saw the metonymic shift in *one* family; here it is **34 of 38 pairs**, and the drift and quadrant results are embedder-independent (bge-m3 agrees with MiniLM on 33/38). This is the strongest passage-grain version of the metonymy claim the project holds.

**The page-grain operation is site-blind.** Three metrics — composition (I6), level (the ascent branch), predictability (F2's uniform compressor) — all say alignment's mark on generated text is a constant register shift, not a response to content. The tonic result.

**The propagation slope** (`propagation.md`), which came out of your reframe and is the capstone. Forcing an improbable word damages the chain in *direction* (p≈2e-08) and by about **1.3%** in *magnitude*. An imposed improbable word propagates no more than one the model sampled itself. This explains the whole series retrospectively: five probes were hunting a downstream trace of something that transmits at one percent.

**Alignment reduces drift in Chinese as in English** (`crosslingual_arms.md`). 25 pairs, same corpus, same rung, language the only manipulation — the design our Chinese work has never had. zh −0.0314, en −0.0205, both stronger on mean_drift, all surviving sentence-count matching, and the sentence-count confound running *against* the finding. The language difference-in-differences is null everywhere. First arm effect this campaign has measured on Chinese generated text.

**The one alignment-specific forced-arm result** (`self_surprisal.md` S4): the aligned model settles when made to say a word alignment *promoted*, and the base does not. It survived the attack I named against it.

## What was withdrawn, and why that matters

**`opening_matched.md` — withdrawn at construction level, not amended.** Your on-the-fly matching design, four controls run, every one survived — and all four were downstream of a defect in what the two arms *are*. The forced word conditions generation but sits in neither the prompt nor the scored text, so forced rows carried an extra word of context. A control can only remove what it compares. The lesson I'd keep: **every control surviving is itself suspicious.**

**The "imposition not demotion" headline — withdrawn.** I named a finding after an ordering of three point estimates and never tested the ordering. All three arm-vs-arm contrasts are non-significant and the ordering inverts under the mean.

**Your offset repair rescued the comparison** (`offset_repair.md`) and the effect *reversed* sign, which is how we learned the "compensation" was the offset entirely.

## What I'd correct in what I told you earlier

The compensation reading is established for **base** models and unestablished for aligned ones (aligned p=.154 and .066 on the two instruments). I overstated it. And "alignment is paradigmatic, not syntagmatic" is too strong: the arm signature survives to the page, the quadrant flow is alignment-specific, and S4 is a real syntagmatic effect. The defensible form is that alignment operates on selection, and the combination axis is unchanged as a *mechanism* while differing in *output* because its input differs.

## Method notes worth keeping

Five things cost real findings today and are now in the seat's memory:

1. **Read the results file, not the producer.** Another module had named my exact invalidation in advance, in a committed file, before I started.
2. **Guard the column, never the row** — and check where the row was *built*, not where it was skipped. A `continue` needed by one derived column silently filtered every column beside it, 23.5% of one arm against 0.0% of another.
3. **When two counts disagree, enumerate the differing members before naming a mechanism.** Two seats produced five wrong attributions of one gap, each a true statement about the sources.
4. **An empty comparison that emits a p-value** is indistinguishable from a null on the page. Caught one in the control a plan had designated decisive.
5. **Impossible agreement is a free check.** Three silent query failures on Chinese data in one day; what caught them was a known answer and a structural impossibility, never the instrument.
