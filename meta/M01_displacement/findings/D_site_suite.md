# Findings D / D2 / D3b: at the substitution sites, what moves is extremity

Split out of `C_to_O_registered_letters.md` on 2026-08-12 (RH's commission), rewritten the same day to be readable on its own: the hypotheses are stated in plain terms rather than by registration number. `REGISTRATIONS.md` remains authoritative for every number; the registration files hold the frozen statistical detail.

## What was asked

The D suite zooms from the whole corpus (C's question) down to the specific sites where substitution happens: minimal pairs of prompts, identical except that one member contains a transgressive element and the other does not. When alignment swaps a word out at these sites, what property of the replacement changes? Three rounds. **D** asked whether the swap moves words toward more positive valence (the registered signed direction) and whether it moves arousal (how activating or calming a word is). **D2** asked the flattening version, in two arms: does the swap reduce valence EXTREMITY, and dominance extremity — the site-level version of what C found corpus-wide. **D3b** asked a deflationary question about D2's result: is the flattening just inherited from the menu? If the pool of available substitute words is on average less extreme, the effect would come from the menu of options, not from a preference among them.

## What was found

**The substitution does not make words nicer, and it does not ride an arousal gradient.** D's signed valence hypothesis is NOT SUPPORTED (paired difference statistic D +0.01272, p 0.8896 — the statistic is a per-pair difference tested against a sign-flip null, i.e. against the distribution you would get if direction were random). The arousal null is strong enough to be QUOTABLE as a null (D −0.02841, p 0.9967): whatever alignment is doing at these sites, it is not selecting calmer words.

**What the substitution does move is extremity, in both arms.** D2: valence-extremity D +0.01525 (p 0.0076) and dominance-extremity D +0.01624 (p 0.0114). The replacement word is systematically less emotionally extreme and less dominance-extreme than the word it replaces.

**And it is not just the menu.** D3b decomposed D2's effect against pool extremity — a bracketing exercise by design, no significance test. The share of the effect attributable to the pool is bounded above, and for dominance the pool component has the OPPOSITE sign to the effect. "It just reflects pool extremity" is dead; whether the pool partially mediates a real preference stays open.

## The limit that travels

Everything here is site-level. It does not license any corpus-level sentence: the bridge from these sites to the general corpus was tested by registration Q and REVERSED (see `Q_bridge.md` — the two frames disagree about direction, and that disagreement is a finding).
